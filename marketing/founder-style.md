# How top founders write posts

The `marketing-expert` agent reads this before drafting anything for X. It is a study of how well-known founders and CEOs post, turned into rules for this account.

**How this was gathered (2026-11-13).** X timelines could not be read directly (they need a login), so this is built from published commentary on these accounts and from the model's knowledge of their widely quoted public posts. It describes patterns, not exact quotes. Before copying a pattern heavily, the founder should look at a few recent posts from that account and check it still holds. Jensen Huang and Mark Zuckerberg post rarely on X; their notes are thinner than the others.

## What each one does

| Founder | How their posts read | What to take |
|---|---|---|
| **Sam Altman** | Lowercase, one or two short sentences, flat and declarative. Announces big things in small words ("we are launching … today"). Understates. Often a single observation with no call to action. Teases with almost no context. | Understatement reads as confidence. Say the thing plainly and stop. |
| **Elon Musk** | Very short, blunt, high volume. One-line product facts, jokes and memes in the same feed. Replies in one or two words. Rarely explains. | A product post can be one fact. Humour is allowed. Brevity is the style. |
| **Mark Zuckerberg** | Casual first person ("I", "we're rolling out"). Product news framed as a personal update, almost always attached to a demo video. Lately a little self-deprecating. | Lead with the demo. Sound like a person showing a thing they made, not a press release. |
| **Jensen Huang** | Rare, upbeat, big simple statements; most of his reach comes from stage moments other people clip. | One large, simple idea per post. Let the visual carry it. |
| **Brian Chesky** | Longer posts and threads. Tells the story behind a design decision; talks about craft and detail. | When there is a real story or a design lesson, a thread earns its length. |
| **Tobi Lütke** | A builder's voice: curious, technical, generous with what he learned. Shares tools and memos. | Teach something. Share the working, not only the result. |
| **Guillermo Rauch** | Ships constantly and shows it: a short clip or screenshot, one line saying what is new, a link. Credits the team. | Every release is a post. The format is clip, one line, link. |
| **Pieter Levels** | Solo builder in public. Plain, lowercase-leaning, real numbers, screenshots, mistakes and all. | Honesty and specifics beat polish for a solo founder. |
| **Paul Graham** | One clear idea per post, short sentences, no decoration. | If a sentence does not carry the idea, cut it. |

## What they have in common

1. **Short.** Most posts are one to three sentences. Length is spent only on stories and lessons.
2. **First person, plain words.** "I built", "we shipped". Words a friend would use out loud.
3. **The visual does the persuading.** The text points at the clip; it does not describe it.
4. **One idea.** Never two announcements in one post.
5. **No marketing furniture.** No hashtags, no "🚀 Excited to announce", no "game-changer", no row of emoji, no exclamation marks doing the work of a fact.
6. **Understated beats hyped.** The bigger the claim, the flatter the delivery.
7. **Specific.** A number, a name, a concrete before and after.
8. **Rarely a hard sell.** The link is there; the ask is light or absent. The account is interesting first.
9. **They reply.** Much of their reach is replies and quote posts, not announcements.

## What not to copy

- **Lowercase** is Sam Altman's signature. On a new account it can read as imitation. Use normal sentence case unless the founder already types that way.
- **Musk's feuds and provocations.** They work for an account with a quarter of a billion followers and cost a small one its credibility.
- **Vague teasing** ("more soon"). It only works when people are already waiting.
- **Their scale claims.** We have no numbers to state. Do not imply any.

## Rules for this account

- Default length: under 200 characters for a post with a video. The video is the content.
- Structure for a product clip: one line that names what the viewer is looking at, then at most two more lines. No summary of the product.
- Put the link in the first reply, with one or two plain sentences on what the product is.
- Sentence case. No hashtags. At most one emoji, usually none.
- Write the claim the video proves, and nothing it does not.
- Offer three hooks every time: one that states what is on screen, one short and dry, one that tells the reason it was built.
